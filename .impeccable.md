## Design Context

### Users

Biomanufacturing process scientists, data analysts, reviewers, and auditors working at laboratory and pilot-production desks. They need to maintain vessel and recipe definitions, inspect imported multichannel sensor series, align batch kinetics against phase-constrained reference curves, investigate deviations, and review evidence without issuing equipment commands.

### Brand Personality

Precise, calm, and evidence-led. The interface should build confidence through traceable measurements and restrained technical language, while making serious deviation states unmistakable.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a light laboratory process-workbench aesthetic with cool white surfaces and leaf-green structural accents. Amber and magenta are reserved for deviation severity and exceptions. Favor dense, scan-oriented layouts with kinetics curves, residual bands, phase regions, and batch comparison as the primary visual language. Do not use gradients, marketing heroes, nested cards, decorative laboratory-vessel illustrations, or atmospheric decoration.

### Design Principles

1. Make measured evidence and phase context visible before summary labels.
2. Keep repeated operational workflows compact, predictable, and keyboard accessible.
3. Reserve saturated color for deviation meaning; keep structural UI quiet.
4. Meet WCAG AA contrast, honor reduced-motion preferences, and prevent mobile overflow.
5. Clearly separate offline analytical recommendations from any real equipment control.
